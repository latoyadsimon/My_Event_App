All-in-One Event Planner App — Product Spec
Oct 30, 2026 · @LaToya
Overview
I want one app that runs my whole event business, from first inquiry to the thank-you note, so I stop juggling a dozen tools. Today I use separate apps for my calendar, invoices, RSVPs, sign-up sheets, inventory spreadsheets, a rented photo booth app and a karaoke queue on paper. Things fall through the cracks between them.
I plan weddings, birthdays, baby and bridal showers, corporate parties, school and church functions, fundraisers and galas, reunions, holiday parties, memorials and community potlucks. Some are paid client events; some are nonprofit or community events where guests donate or bring food.
Vision: every event lives in one "Event Hub." Everything about it (client, guests, money, gear, vendors, schedule, photos, music) hangs off that hub and talks to everything else. When a guest RSVPs, the headcount updates the catering order, the seating chart and the potluck plan automatically.
What success looks like:
• I can see every upcoming event, its balance due and its open tasks on one screen.
• A new event goes from inquiry to signed contract and deposit in under 15 minutes.
• No equipment is double-booked or lost.
• On event day, my staff and I run everything from our phones, even with bad Wi-Fi.
• Guests think the invitations, photo booth and karaoke are fun and polished, and they come back.

Event hub, calendar and scheduling
The home screen is a dashboard; each event is a hub with tabs for every module below.
Dashboard (my home screen)
• Today and this week: events, site visits, tastings, calls, deliveries and pickups.
• Alerts: unpaid balances, unsigned contracts, low RSVP responses, gear not returned, tasks overdue.
• Pipeline: inquiries, proposals sent, booked, completed. Monthly revenue booked vs. collected.
Event hub (one per event)
• Event type, date(s), times, venue (with map, parking notes, load-in door, Wi-Fi password), client, budget, guest count, theme and colors.
• Status: Inquiry, Proposal, Booked, In planning, Final week, Event day, Wrap-up, Closed.
• Tabs: Overview, Tasks, Timeline, Guests, Money, Vendors, Equipment, Potluck, Photos, Karaoke, Files, Messages, Notes.
• Multi-day events (wedding weekend, conference) with sub-events: rehearsal dinner, ceremony, reception, brunch.
• Duplicate an event or save it as a template.
Calendar
• Month, week, day and list views; color-coded by event type and status.
• Two-way sync with Google Calendar, Apple/iCloud and Outlook.
• Holds: soft-hold a date for a prospect that auto-expires after X days.
• Conflict warnings when I or a staff member is double-booked, or travel time between venues is too short.
• Public availability page so prospects can see open dates and book a consult (like Calendly), with buffer times and daily limits.
• Automatic reminders by email and text to clients, staff and vendors.
Staff scheduling
• Create shifts per event (setup, service, teardown) with roles: coordinator, KJ, photo booth attendant, server, bartender.
• Staff set their availability; I assign shifts and they accept or decline in the app.
• Clock in and out with GPS check at the venue; hours roll into a payroll export.
• Each staff member sees their own call sheet: address, arrival time, dress code, contact on site, duties.
Client management
The sales side should take a prospect from inquiry to booked with almost no retyping.
1. Inquiry form I can embed on my website, Instagram and Facebook: event type, date, guest count, budget range, services wanted. It creates a lead automatically and checks my calendar for conflicts.
2. Lead tracking with source (referral, Instagram, Google, The Knot), follow-up reminders and canned email/text replies.
3. Proposals built from my service packages and add-ons (photo booth, karaoke, rentals), with photos, optional upgrades the client can tick, and a live total with tax.
4. Contracts generated from templates with merge fields (names, date, venue, price, cancellation policy). E-signature for both parties, timestamped and stored as PDF.
5. Deposit collected right after signing, on the same screen.
6. Client portal where the client sees their timeline, payment schedule, guest list, mood board, documents and a message thread with me. They can approve things (menu, layout, final headcount) with one tap, and each approval is logged.
Client records keep history across events (a client who books a baby shower and later a first birthday), notes, preferences, allergies, important dates and birthdays for marketing reminders.
Questionnaires I can send at set points (for example, "Wedding details" 90 days out), with answers feeding straight into the event hub.
Planning tools
These are the tools I use every day between booking and event day.
Templates and checklists
• Checklist templates per event type, with tasks scheduled relative to the event date ("Order cake: 30 days before"). Dates recalculate if the event moves.
• Assign tasks to me, staff, the client or a vendor; due-date reminders; subtasks; attachments.
Run-of-show (event-day timeline)
• Minute-by-minute schedule: setup, doors, grand entrance, toasts, cake cutting, first dance, karaoke start, last call, teardown.
• Each item has an owner, location and cue notes. Shareable versions for client, vendors and staff (vendors see only their parts).
• Printable one-page version and a live mode on event day (see Day-of mode).
Budget
• Budget by category with estimated vs. actual, linked to vendor invoices and payments.
• My profit view: client price minus vendor costs, staff hours and gear wear.
Vendors
• Directory of my preferred vendors with category, rates, contacts, ratings, insurance certificate (COI) expiration alerts and past events together.
• Per event: booked vendors, contract status, payment due, arrival times.
Floor plans and seating
• Drag-and-drop floor plan: tables (round, banquet, cocktail), dance floor, stage, buffet, photo booth, karaoke station, power outlets, exits.
• Upload the venue's floor plan as a background and set the scale.
• Seating chart where I drag guests to seats; flags for conflicts ("don't seat near"), meal choices and allergies per table.
• Printable escort cards, table numbers and place cards.
Inspiration
• Mood boards with uploads and Pinterest links, color palette and theme notes the client can comment on.
Invitations, RSVPs and guest list
Invitations are the first thing guests see, so they must look beautiful and work on any phone with no app download.
Designing invitations
• Template gallery by event type, plus my own branded templates. Edit fonts, colors, photos, wording; optional animated envelope opening and background music.
• Save-the-dates, invitations, reminders, change notices and thank-you cards all from the same design.
• Print-ready PDF export for paper invitations, with a QR code that links to the online RSVP.
Sending
• Send by email, text (SMS), WhatsApp, or a shareable link and QR code for social media and flyers.
• Import guest lists from CSV, Excel, phone contacts or Google Contacts; the client can add guests in their portal.
• Households and plus-ones: one invite per household, with named guests and an allowed number of extra guests.
• Track delivered, opened, RSVP'd; one-tap reminders to non-responders.
• Scheduled sends and automatic "one week to go" reminders with directions, parking and dress code.
RSVP page (event website)
• Yes / No / Maybe, number attending, meal choice, dietary needs and allergies, song request, custom questions.
• Event details, map, schedule, hotel block, registry or donation link, FAQ, add-to-calendar button.
• Links straight into the potluck sign-up, donations and karaoke song requests.
• Optional password or private-link mode; public mode for community events.
Guest list
• Live headcount by response and meal choice, feeding seating, catering and potluck totals.
• Tags (family, work, bride's side, VIP, kids), notes and gift tracking for thank-you cards.
• Check-in on event day by QR code scan or name search.
Payments, ticketing and donations
All money in and out should be tracked per event and roll up into my business totals.
Client payments
• Invoices generated from the signed proposal, with a payment schedule (for example, 30% deposit, 40% at 60 days, 30% at 14 days).
• Pay by card, Apple Pay, Google Pay, ACH bank transfer; record cash, check, Venmo, Zelle and Cash App manually.
• Automatic reminders before and after due dates; optional late fees; autopay option for clients.
• Option to pass the card processing fee on to the client.
• Refunds, partial refunds, credits and security deposits for rentals (hold and release).
• Tap-to-pay on my phone for on-site add-ons (extra hour of karaoke, extra prints).
Ticketing (for public events and fundraisers)
• Ticket tiers (general, VIP, table of 8, early bird with an end date), promo codes, capacity limits, waitlist.
• QR tickets emailed to buyers and scanned at the door.
Donations
• Donation page per event with suggested amounts, custom amount, recurring option and "cover the fees" checkbox.
• Live fundraising thermometer I can put on a screen at the event.
• Text-to-give or QR code on tables; paddle raise and silent auction bidding from guests' phones.
• Donor list with receipts that include the tax-deductible amount for nonprofit clients.
• Memorial and "in lieu of flowers" funds, and cash gift funds (honeymoon fund, baby fund) for showers and weddings.
Bookkeeping
• Expenses per event (receipt photo capture), vendor payouts and staff pay.
• Sales tax by location; reports by month, event type and payment method.
• Sync with QuickBooks; payouts through Stripe or Square.
Potluck organizer
The potluck tool's job is to prevent twelve bags of chips and no main dishes.
• Slots by category with target quantities scaled to headcount: mains, sides, salads, desserts, drinks, bread, plus supplies (plates, cups, ice, napkins, serving spoons, extension cords).
• Auto-suggested amounts: "For 80 guests, we need about 6 mains, 8 sides, 5 desserts." Updates as RSVPs change.
• Guest sign-up from the RSVP page or a standalone link: pick a slot, name the dish, number of servings, and tick tags (vegetarian, vegan, gluten-free, dairy-free, nut-free, halal, kosher, spicy).
• Duplicate warning if someone signs up for a dish already taken ("3 people are bringing mac and cheese — want to pick something else?").
• Allergen summary for the organizer; printable food labels and tent cards for each dish with ingredients and allergens.
• Reminders to sign-ups 3 days and 1 day before, with drop-off time and whether to bring dishes hot or cold.
• Equipment needs: sign-ups can flag "needs an outlet" or "needs a warming tray," feeding the equipment list and floor plan.
• Volunteer slots in the same tool: setup, serving, cleanup, greeting, kids' table.
• Leftover and dish return tracking: who brought which container, so Aunt Linda gets her casserole dish back.
• Recipe sharing after the event: guests can post recipes to the event page.
Equipment and inventory
I own a lot of gear and also rent some out, so I need to know where every item is and whether it's free on a given date.
Catalog
• Every item or item group: photo booth kits, printers, backdrops, karaoke speakers, mics, mixers, TVs, lighting, linens, centerpieces, chafing dishes, tables, chairs, tents, signage, decor.
• Photo, quantity owned, storage location (shelf/bin), replacement cost, rental price, serial number, purchase date.
• Kits/bundles: "Photo Booth Kit A" = camera, ring light, iPad, printer, stand, backdrop, props bin. Reserving the kit reserves every piece.
• Consumables with stock levels and reorder alerts: printer paper and ink, magnet sheets, sleeves, batteries, tape, zip ties.
Availability and reservations
• Reserve gear to an event; the app blocks double-booking and shows available quantity by date, including cleaning/turnaround buffer.
• Warning when I quote more chairs than I own, with an option to add a rental from a partner vendor.
Pack lists and check-out/check-in
• Auto-generated pack list per event from reserved gear plus fixed items ("always bring: extension cords, first aid kit, gaffer tape").
• Scan QR or barcode labels to check items out to the van and back in afterward. Missing items are flagged before we leave the venue.
• Damage and loss reports with photos; option to bill the client from the security deposit.
Maintenance
• Service history, cleaning status, "out of service" flag, and reminders (replace mic batteries, clean printer heads every 500 prints).
• Usage reports: which items earn the most and which never leave the shelf.
Rentals to the public (stretch goal): customers browse my rental catalog, pick dates, pay, sign a rental agreement and choose pickup or delivery.
Photo booth and photo magnets
I want to replace my rented booth software with my own, running on an iPad or Android tablet (and ideally a laptop with a DSLR), tied to the event.
Booth mode (guest-facing kiosk)
• Locked kiosk mode so guests can't leave the app; attendant unlocks with a PIN.
• Big "Tap to start" welcome screen branded per event; countdown with sound; live preview.
• Capture modes: single photo, 3- or 4-shot strip, GIF, boomerang, short video message (guestbook), burst.
• Overlays and frames designed per event (names, date, logo, theme), plus digital props and filters. Optional green-screen background replacement and AI background removal.
• Retake option, then share: text, email, AirDrop/QR code to download. Optional data capture (email/phone opt-in) for corporate clients, with a consent checkbox.
Printing
• Print to dye-sub photo printers (DNP, Mitsubishi, Canon Selphy) in 4x6, 2x6 strips and 2x3 or 3x3.5 magnet sizes.
• Set copies per session, print limits, and "print one for the guestbook" automatically.
• Print queue with status, reprint, and paper/ink counter that updates inventory.
Photo magnet mode
• An attendant workflow for magnet stations: photographer shoots roaming candids from a phone or camera, the app pulls photos into a queue, attendant picks and crops to the magnet template, prints, then a guest picks it up at the station.
• Guests can also upload their own phone photos by scanning a QR code to request a magnet.
• Magnet frame templates per event; count of magnets printed vs. package limit, with an upsell button for more.
Gallery and sharing
• Live gallery website and slideshow for a TV or projector at the event, updating as photos are taken.
• Moderation: attendant can hide a photo before it appears on the slideshow.
• Post-event gallery for the client: download all in full resolution, guest uploads in the same place, expiry date I control.
• Analytics: sessions, prints, shares, emails captured (useful for corporate clients).
• Works offline; uploads sync when Wi-Fi returns.
Karaoke jockey (KJ)
The KJ module replaces my paper slips and clipboard, and keeps the rotation fair.
Guest side (phone, no app)
• Scan the QR code on tables to open the song request page.
• Search the song catalog by title or artist; request a song with their name or a stage name; duets and group songs with multiple singers.
• See their place in line and get a text when they're 2 singers away.
• Favorites and "my history" for regulars at recurring events.
KJ side (tablet or laptop)
• Live queue with drag-to-reorder, skip, hold, "singer not here" and move to end.
• Fair rotation mode: each singer gets one turn per round before anyone sings twice; first-timers and VIPs (birthday person, bride) can be bumped up.
• Content filters per event: clean versions only, block explicit songs, block certain songs, cap song length.
• Song catalog management: import my licensed karaoke library (CDG, MP3+G, MP4) and/or connect to a licensed streaming karaoke provider. I need the developer to advise on licensing.
• Playback control with key change and tempo change, crossfade, background music between singers, and a mic level reminder.
• Second-screen output: lyrics on the main TV, and a "Now singing / Up next" screen for the crowd.
• Announcements and contests: judges score from their phones, audience votes, results on screen.
After the event
• Report of songs sung, number of singers, most popular songs. Optional videos of performances added to the event gallery with the singer's consent.
Day-of mode and after the event
Day-of mode is a simplified, big-button phone view for the event itself.
• Live run-of-show with a "now / next" view, countdowns to each cue, and a tap to mark items done or running late. If something slips, the following times shift and the team is notified.
• Team chat and push alerts per event ("Cake is here," "Speeches in 5 minutes"), with walkie-talkie style voice notes.
• Guest check-in by QR or name search, live attendance count, walk-in add.
• Vendor arrival check-in with timestamp.
• Emergency info at one tap: venue manager, nearest hospital, client's point of contact, allergy list.
• Incident log with photos (spills, damage, injuries) for insurance.
• Full offline support: everything for the event downloads in advance and syncs later.
After the event
• Automatic thank-you emails to guests with the photo gallery link; thank-you note tracker for the client (who gave what).
• Review requests to the client a few days later, with links to Google, Yelp, The Knot and WeddingWire.
• Feedback survey to client and guests.
• Close-out checklist: final invoice paid, security deposit released, gear checked in, staff hours approved, vendor payments sent.
• Event report: revenue, costs, profit, attendance, photo booth and karaoke stats, donations raised.
• Marketing: anniversary and birthday reminders ("Your baby's first birthday is in 2 months") and opt-in email lists.
Technical requirements and phasing
I'm not technical, so these are my needs; I'd like your recommendations on how to meet them.
Platforms
• iOS and Android apps for me and staff; a full web app for desktop work (proposals, floor plans, reports).
• Guest, client, vendor and donor pages are mobile web, no download.
• Photo booth and KJ run on tablets (iPad first) with optional laptop support for DSLR cameras and pro audio.
Must-haves
• Offline-first for day-of, booth and KJ modes, with reliable sync.
• Fast: guest pages load in under 2 seconds on a phone.
• Secure payments (PCI handled by Stripe or Square; I never store card numbers).
• Privacy: guest data, photos and minors' images handled carefully; consent for photo sharing and data capture; easy data deletion requests.
• Accessibility for guest pages (screen readers, large text, color contrast) and English plus Spanish to start.
• My branding everywhere guests and clients look (logo, colors, custom domain like events.mybusiness.com).
• Automatic backups and an export of all my data.
